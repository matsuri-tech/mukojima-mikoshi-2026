ABOUT

Shoulder the Shrine

A mikoshi is a portable Shinto shrine that a neighborhood carries on their shoulders through the streets — chanting, swaying and cheering together. The Mukojima 2 & 3-chome town association is welcoming a few of our hotel guests to the Ushijima Shrine Festival, when the association's great mikoshi is carried through the streets of Mukojima — an old shitamachi district in the shadow of Tokyo Skytree. You'll put on a happi coat, line up shoulder-to-shoulder with the local bearers — our staff will be carrying too — and carry the shrine yourself. No experience needed — the team shows you how.

NOTES

Good to know

  • Limited spotsApplying doesn't confirm your place — spots are limited (around 20 guests) and we select among applicants. Please apply by 5:00 p.m. on Wednesday, Sep 16 (Japan time). We email the selected guests by 7:00 p.m. the same evening with the meeting point and details. If you have not heard from us by then, you were not selected this time.
  • Who can joinThis event is for guests staying at our properties in the Mukojima / Oshiage (Tokyo Skytree) area — you'll need your reservation number to apply. It's a physical event, so it's for healthy participants roughly ages 15 and up.
  • Your scheduleOne day only — Sunday, September 20. Meet at 9:15 a.m.; the carrying runs from 10:00 a.m. to around 1:00 p.m. You join the morning session and finish at the lunch break — the festival itself continues into the evening without the guest group.
  • What you wearA rental happi coat is included in the fee — you just slip it over your own clothes, so no changing is needed (and there is no changing room). Ordinary comfortable clothes are perfect: a T-shirt and shorts are fine. Please keep the happi on the whole time you're with the mikoshi, and hand it back to our staff when the group breaks up.
  • ShoesSneakers are fine — no special footwear (tabi) needed. Flat, closed shoes you can walk and stand in for a few hours are perfect. Sandals, flip-flops, heels and slippery soles aren't safe for carrying.
  • How the carrying worksBearers take turns: you carry short stretches, swap out, and rest — there are breaks with drinks along the route. The guest session ends at the lunch break, around 1:00 p.m., when you return your happi to our staff.
  • Fee & what you take homeThe participation fee is ¥10,000 per person, paid in cash on the day. It covers the happi coat rental, a traditional tenugui hand towel and an obi sash — both yours to keep — plus drinks during the breaks and lunch. The rental happi goes back to our staff when the group breaks up.
  • LuggagePlease come as hands-free as you can — a small bag at most. There is no luggage storage at the festival.
  • Follow the crewThis is a real neighborhood tradition we're being welcomed into. The association's officials run the procession — please follow their guidance and our staff's at all times; anyone who doesn't will be asked to return the happi and leave.
  • Safety rulesNever climb on the mikoshi or its carrying poles. Bare torsos are not allowed — keep the happi on. No alcohol during the procession — fighting or drunken behavior means immediate removal.
  • Getting therePlease come by train or on foot — there is no parking at the festival. The meeting point is in Mukojima, a short walk from the Oshiage / Tokyo Skytree area. Smoking only in designated areas.
  • TimePlease keep the morning of Sunday, Sep 20 open. The exact meeting point and time go only to selected guests, by email; if you're running late on the day, contact the staff member named in that message.
  • Photos & videoBy joining, you agree that we may take photos and video of the group for our website, press and social media — this event is only for guests who are happy with that. When you take your own photos, remember local families and children are everywhere, so please ask before pointing your camera at people.
  • WeatherLight rain doesn't stop the festival. If severe weather forces a cancellation, we'll let you know by message on your booking platform before the meeting time. It can still be hot in September — bring water.
